What the core needs from the SpinalVoodoo yardstick

Logic, Voodoo 2
65K–75K
LUT6. Voodoo 1 core is 35K–40K; second TMU, cmdFifo and memory FIFO add half again; PCI target, DDR3 controller and video add 10K.
Block RAM
≥ 10 Mbit
Texture caches per TMU and deep FIFOs. This is what fixes the small-triangle stall measured on the DE10.
Core clock
90 MHz
Voodoo 2 native. The closed-source ECP5 Voodoo 1 hit 100 MHz on a slower fabric.
I/O
3.3V bank
About 50 pins for PCI behind 5V bus switches, plus DDR3, PSRAM and video. Roughly 225 pins total.
Tools
free
No per-seat licence. This rules out Kintex-7 325T and Zynq 7Z035/7Z045 in Vivado.

Recommendation: T113-S3. The $21 premium buys the host-less self-test path, which is what makes bring-up of a register-level Voodoo tractable. The microcontroller option was dropped on 2026-09-16: the FPGA boots from its own QSPI flash, JTAG programs it, updates arrive over PCI or from the T113, and the FPGA drives the relays, so a basic card without the T113 carries no companion CPU at all. Memory trade-off what the K26 was quietly paying for

With UltraRAM (Zynq UltraScale+)

18 Mbit on chip holds two 640×480×16 colour buffers plus depth with single-cycle latency. The whole Voodoo 2 framebuffer lives inside the FPGA.

With 7-series block RAM

The 200T's 13 Mbit holds one colour buffer plus depth. The colour buffers go to HyperRAM (ISSI IS66WVH8M8BLL, chosen 2026-09-16) with textures in DDR3. Workable, and the ModRetro M64 does the same with PSRAM, but it is a design cost the cheaper chips carry.

Decision procedure let the KV260 port pick the chip

  1. Port SpinalVoodoo to the KV260 kit
    $249, kept as a dev board whatever the outcome. Port Avalon to AXI and the DE10 runtime to the A53. This yields the real Voodoo 2 LUT count and real memory latency numbers.
  2. In parallel, synthesise the core through Gowin EDA
    A day's work, no hardware. Read the utilisation, Fmax and BRAM it reports for the GW5AST-138. The bare chip is $152 to $189 at LCSC, so the Gowin route only pays through Sipeed's $69 Tang Mega 138K module, which also removes the T113 because the chip's hard RISC-V runs Linux. If the core fits at 90 MHz with depth in HyperRAM, a $130 module-based card is real.
  3. Choose
    Core under 70K LUT6 and one-chip simplicity wanted: Zynq 7Z030. Otherwise Artix-7 200T or Kintex-7 160T with a T113-S3, at $175 to $195 a board. Gowin on the Sipeed module only if step 2 came back clean and single-source supply is acceptable.
